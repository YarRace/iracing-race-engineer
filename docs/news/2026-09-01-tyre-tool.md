# Tyre Tool: what the tread edges say, and what they were saying wrong

The tread tells you two things without any guesswork. A hot inner edge means
there is more negative camber than the corner is using. A hot middle means the
tyre is crowned, which is pressure. Both are physics and neither depends on the
car.

That reading now lives in two places: a card in the dashboard, and a widget over
the game — because the moment you actually want it is leaving the pits after
changing camber, and that is exactly when the second screen is behind you.

There are no per-car target pressures here, and there will not be invented ones.
There is no source that says what a Ferrari 499P should run, and a number with no
provenance is worse than a blank. Instead the tool leans on two honest things:
the physics above, and **your own fastest stint** — pressures are now saved with
each stint, so "aim for what was on the car when you were quickest" becomes real
as the stints pile up.

## Three things that were wrong, all found by measuring

**The whole tyre section was reading a constant.** Temperatures came from the
carcass channels. In a real session those return **two distinct values across
51,797 samples** — a placeholder, not telemetry. The surface channels return
about eleven thousand. Spread was therefore always zero and the verdict always
"even": that part of every report meant nothing. Now it reads the surface, with
the carcass kept as a fallback.

**Inner and outer were swapped.** The channels name the sides of the tyre in the
*car's* frame — the .ibt header says "LF tire left surface temperature" — so on
the left-hand wheels the inner edge is R, and on the right-hand wheels it is L.
It was the other way round, which means the camber advice came out backwards:
"take some out" where the answer was "put some in". Confirmed twice over: by the
channel description, and by 32 of your own sessions, where in all fourteen with a
readable difference the hotter side was the one facing the middle of the car.
Not one counterexample.

**One threshold lived in two files** and the numbers had drifted apart. Fifteen
wheels out of ninety-six were getting "even" from one module and "too much
camber" from the other — and you could see both, one in the AI review and one on
the card. There is one verdict now, in one place.

## And one thing that is still not right

The threshold is not universal, and that is measured rather than suspected. It
fires on **2% of Ferrari wheels and 39% of Super Formula Lights wheels** — twenty
times the rate. So it describes a car's habit, not a law.

It has been left alone anyway. There is telemetry for three cars, one of them a
single session, and car and track are not separable in it: the Ferrari only ever
ran Road Atlanta, the SF Lights only Road America. Swapping one non-universal
constant for another would only look like progress. What did change is that the
measurement is now a command — `python tools/measure_tyres.py` — which prints the
distribution per car and says "this threshold has drifted" out loud when the
rates diverge.
